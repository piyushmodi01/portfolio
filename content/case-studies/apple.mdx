<Chapter
  number="01"
  title="The Environment"
  intro="Most products fight for a user's time. This one is part of how thousands of employees do their jobs every day."
/>

<Prose>
As a contract designer, I work on an internal platform thousands of employees use every day to get their jobs done. I sit inside the product team, working alongside engineering and PMs running several projects at once.

The people using this aren't strangers. They're colleagues doing real work. That changes everything. There's no marketing, no onboarding, no star rating — you earn trust by being useful, over and over.
</Prose>

<Chapter
  number="02"
  title="The System I Contribute To"
  intro="Every product in this org builds on a shared design system."
/>

<Pull accent>
  One decision at the foundation shows up in everything built on top of it.
</Pull>

<Prose>
I helped build and maintain the org's shared design system — the foundation every product, screen, and component is built on. Getting it right there matters the most, because every product inherits from it.
</Prose>

<Chapter
  title="Raising the Bar on Accessibility"
  intro="Accessibility has always been design's responsibility. The goal was to make it everyone's habit."
/>

<Prose>
I built several products used across the team to evaluate designs and products against Apple's accessibility standards, and to keep improving them over time.

What started as a way to check my own work became something PMs ran before design reviews and engineers ran before code review. Accessibility stopped being design's job alone.
</Prose>

<Pull accent>
  Accessibility is design's job. These tools just made it easier for everyone else to participate.
</Pull>

<Chapter
  number="04"

  intro="Several products, shipped in parallel."

<Prose>
Several products shipped or moved to engineering during this engagement, all under NDA. I focused on understanding the business, researching deeply, building and testing continuously, and delivering work that didn't just help people do their jobs — it made them say wow.
</Prose>
